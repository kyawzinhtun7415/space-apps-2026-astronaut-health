Astronaut Health Twin

How am I doing today, what is abnormal, and what should I do about it?

Simulated sample data

This prototype shows how a crew member far from Earth could judge their own health readings alone. Pick a sample day, or enter your own readings. Each reading is compared with your own pre-launch baseline, with what small studies measured in space, and with a published clinical chart. Then you get a summary of how you are doing, what changed, and a plan for what to do and who to tell.

Words used on this page
Pre-launch baseline
Your own numbers, measured on Earth before the mission.
Space shift
A change that a study measured in crews during flight. It is an average for a small group.
Clinical chart
A published table of normal and abnormal values that clinicians use on Earth.
NEWS2
A UK hospital chart that gives each vital sign a score from 0 to 3. Higher means further from normal.
Flagged
A published chart puts the value outside its normal range. High or low says which side.
mmHg
The unit for blood pressure. The top number is the pressure when the heart beats, the bottom number when it rests.
mg/dL
The unit for blood sugar used here.
Core temperature
The temperature inside the body, not at the skin.

            What the labels mean

              Concept prototype with simulated data. It does not detect disease or clear anyone for a task. Not a medical device.

              The summary, the pattern notes, the steps for what to do and who to tell, the task check, the symptom list and the cut-offs behind them are examples by this team. They are not NASA's rules. Score bands are quoted from the NEWS2 chart of the Royal College of Physicians. This tool does not reproduce that chart or calculate a NEWS2 total, and NEWS2 was designed for hospital patients, so it is a rough guide here. The tolerances behind "expected in space" and the act-now lines for pulse and low blood sugar are this team's own.
